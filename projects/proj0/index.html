Becoming Friends
with My Camera

Tim — Intro to Computer Vision and Computational Photography, Fall 2026

FOCAL 24–85mm APERTURE ƒ/1.8 SUBJECT Perspective & Center of Projection

A camera doesn't just record a scene — where you stand and how far you zoom quietly rewrites its geometry. This page walks through three experiments in that relationship: a selfie taken two different ways, a building facade compressed and stretched, and a dolly zoom that pulls both tricks at once.

01

The Selfie: Wrong Way vs. Right Way

Same face, same framing — one shot from close range, one taken from several feet back and zoomed in to match. Compare what happens to the proportions of the face.

Why does this happen? (edit me) Up close, the nose and eyes sit much nearer to the lens than the ears and jaw, so nearby features project larger onto the sensor than far ones — the classic wide-angle "big nose" effect. Stepping back and zooming in flattens that difference: every part of the face is now roughly the same distance from the camera, so relative proportions look natural again. The lesson is that flattering portraits are less about the lens itself and more about camera-to-subject distance.
02

Architectural Perspective Compression

The same trick in reverse, aimed at a building facade: zoomed-in from a distance versus wide-angle up close, matched so the building reads at the same size in both.

Why does this happen? (edit me) A long focal length narrows the field of view, so the background and foreground occupy a much smaller range of angles — objects at different depths end up looking similar in size, "compressing" the scene. Standing close with a wide lens does the opposite: near and far surfaces subtend very different angles, so depth and converging lines are exaggerated. Zoom controls perspective indirectly, by controlling how far away you have to stand to fill the frame.
03

The Dolly Zoom

Moving the camera back while zooming in, timed so the subject stays the same size — the "Vertigo shot." The background does all the moving.
